Design for the phone first: a practical guide for businesses in Cameroon

Most of your customers meet your business on a small screen and a limited data bundle. Build for that visitor first.

By TENING BORIS20 October 20264 min read

When a business owner shows me their website, they usually open it on a laptop. When their customers look at it, they are usually holding a phone, often on mobile data that they pay for by the megabyte. A website that is designed for the first situation and merely "shrinks" for the second will lose people.

Start from the visitor's question

Someone on a phone wants one of a handful of things: what you offer, roughly what it costs, where you are, and how to reach you. On a mobile-first site, those answers appear in the first screen or two, with no hunting through menus.

  • Say what you do in one sentence at the top of the homepage.
  • Make contact one tap away. A WhatsApp button and a click-to-call phone number beat a long form for many customers.
  • Show your location with a written address and a link to a map, not just an embedded map that is heavy to load.

Respect the data bundle

Images are usually the heaviest part of a page. A single photo straight from a modern phone can be several megabytes; the same photo prepared for the web can be a small fraction of that and look identical on screen.

  • Resize images to the size they are actually displayed at.
  • Use modern formats like WebP, with a JPEG fallback for older browsers.
  • Load images below the first screen only when the visitor scrolls to them.
  • Be selective with sliders, background videos and chat widgets. Each one adds weight.

Make it comfortable to use with a thumb

Buttons should be large enough to tap without zooming, with space between them. Text should be readable without pinching. And never disable zoom: some visitors need it, and blocking it makes a site harder to use, not easier.

Test on a real, ordinary phone

Browser tools that simulate a phone are useful, but they run on a powerful computer with a fast connection. Before launch, open the site on a mid-range Android phone using mobile data. If it feels slow or awkward to you, it will feel worse to a customer who has never heard of you.
