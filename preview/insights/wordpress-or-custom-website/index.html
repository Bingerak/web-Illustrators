WordPress or a custom-coded website? How to choose

Both can be excellent. The right choice depends on who will update the site and how often.

By TENING BORIS13 October 20264 min read

"Should my website be on WordPress?" is one of the first questions clients ask me. Both WordPress and a custom-coded site can be fast, attractive and secure. The better choice depends less on technology and more on how you will use the site after launch.

Choose WordPress when your team publishes often

WordPress gives you an editor where you can add pages, write news, upload photos and manage products without touching code. It suits:

  • Shops and catalogues that add or change products regularly.
  • Schools, training centres and associations that publish news and events.
  • Teams where several people need to edit content.

The trade-off is upkeep. WordPress, its theme and its plugins need regular updates, and every extra plugin adds weight and another thing that can break. A good WordPress build uses as few plugins as possible and comes with a maintenance plan.

Choose a custom-coded site when content changes rarely

A hand-coded site, built with HTML, CSS and a little JavaScript, has no database and no plugins to update. It suits:

  • Professional and service businesses whose pages change a few times a year.
  • Landing pages for a campaign or product launch.
  • Portfolios like this one.

These sites are usually very fast, cheap to host and have a small security surface. The trade-off is that text changes go through a developer, or through a simple content file.

Three questions to decide

  1. Who will update the site, and how often? Weekly by your team points to WordPress. A few times a year points to custom code.
  2. Do you need a shop, bookings or member accounts? WordPress has mature, tested tools for these.
  3. Who will maintain it in two years? Plan for updates and backups whichever you choose.

Not sure which fits your business? Tell me how you plan to use the site and I will recommend one, with reasons.
